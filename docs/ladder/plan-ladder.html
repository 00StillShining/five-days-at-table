Rung 2 · Pushed

Three studies, one axis each. Everything not on a study's own axis stays exactly as Simple left it, so each claim can be falsified on its own rather than as part of a bundle. Each is stated against Simple, never against another study.

Study A — the scale (geometry only, still grayscale)

Claim: deriving the arc's range from the band rather than from zero is what makes this instrument able to answer its own question. plan.json's kcal band is [1649, 1730] — a span of 4.7% of its own ceiling.

vs Simple — Simple drew a needle over an unmarked ring and printed the figure beneath. A1 keeps that and adds a printed zone: measured live on this page, the band prints as 8.4° of a 270° arc — a target you cannot see the needle enter or leave. A2 puts the band edges at 37.5% and 62.5%, so the target owns 67.5° and the two caution zones and two redlines own the rest, and the geometry is identical on all five dials, which is what lets a row be read as a row. Cost: the dial's minimum is not zero. That is what an oil-temperature gauge does, and the minimum tick carries its own real numeral, so nothing is hidden.

Study B — the case row (proportion only)

Claim: the chapter's ratio is taken off CASE DIAMETERS, so it has to survive all the way down to the visible dial — which means every inset between the column and the face has to be a percentage of the column, not a constant.

vs Simple — Simple already carried the ratio, because ch.17 §7 puts it on the first rung. This study is about what a gutter does to it, and it cost two rounds to get right. Measured in the browser, and the size of the error depends on how large the constant is against the column, so it is quoted twice: calc(var(--cd-dial-1) - 1rem) renders 1.14 : 1 rank-one to outboard on the shipped 60rem row and 2.02 : 1 on this narrower bench — a flat 1rem is simply a bigger share of a 16.8% column than of a 24.2% one. Moving the gutter to a CONSTANT INSET fixes the COLUMNS exactly (0.694 / 0.872 / 1) and still renders the DIALS at 0.635 / 0.847 / 1 on the shipped row and 0.601 / 0.833 / 1 here — and a reader looks at dials, not at columns. Only when every inset in the chain — the housing's margin, its own padding and the bezel's width — is a PERCENTAGE of the column does the dial ratio come out at 0.6942 / 0.8720 / 1 / 0.8720 / 0.6942 on the shipped row, against the chapter's own 0.6942 / 0.8719. Switch the selector and read the caption.

Rung 4 · Signature

ch.17 §7: "the crown, pulled and held past its own hard stop for 350ms of linear fill … trades the entire cluster's finish … while the five-dial row and every present value hold exactly still."

FD-5's own version of that trade is a fact, not a finish. Hold the crown and the board re-commissions its scope between one plated week and the executing fortnight. Because the fortnight is week A run twice (D5, "a-twice"), the reading and the band double together, so the ratio is identical and the needle cannot move — while every printed figure re-casts. A value redrawn is still the same fact.

Hold the crown for 350ms (or focus it and hold Space). Watch the numerals, not the needle. Lifting before 350ms cancels and the fill collapses on the next frame.

What this rung is not allowed to do

Change a value. The re-commission is a change of frame, and the arithmetic proves it: (value × 2) / (band × 2) ≡ value / band. model.test.ts asserts that identity for all five channels, both covers, to nine decimal places — so the needle's stillness is a theorem, not an animation someone remembered to suppress.
