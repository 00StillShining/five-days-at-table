FD-5 · LIST — the escalation ladder

20 IRREDUCIBLE · the PRESTO DL 400 language, on the screen a shopper holds in one hand under 750–1500 lux. Four rungs, every one of them running against the real trip the codec carries. Grey is not restraint here: this world's hue count is zero except live, by its own section 2, and live is spent on exactly one thing — a window that is genuinely open.

Rung 1 · Simple

The doctrine's grayscale ramp, real data, no gradient, no shadow stack, no texture, no spring, no sound. Values snap frame to frame. Every state below is driven, not drawn: pick one and the rung really enters it.

Rung 2 · Pushed

Four studies, one axis each. Everything not on a study's axis stays exactly at Simple, so each claim can be falsified on its own.

Study A — the plate (material only)

Claim: on a world whose whole palette spans #7A7B7D to #ECECEA, a plate is told apart from its mount by its machining, not by its value. Measured, the void and the epoxy body sit 1.54:1 apart — below every threshold there is. The ceramic recipe plus a nickel rim and a real cast is what makes the same two hexes read as two objects.

vs Simple: the Simple rung's plate is background:#1f1f1f and reads as a box. Same information, same ink, same geometry — the second tile reads as a part.

Study B — the seated press (geometry only)

Claim: the tick target is not a checkbox; it is section 3's domed oval paddle, and it has to sit in something. The socket is the live well (#121213, 6.81:1 against the plate) — the one near-black value this world owns, and the reason forty-four rows read as a machined register rather than a list of rows.

vs Simple: the box and the paddle carry identical semantics. Only the third one answers §5.7 — “controls look operable … mistakable for a static icon = failed.”

Study C — the run-out (motion only)

Claim: the undo window has to be visible as a quantity, and how it arrives at zero is the difference between a report and a slam. Section 9's third failure mode is “slam-shut — the run-out ends in a single frame instead of the 1800ms taper”. Press each and watch: same 1800ms, three different truths about what is happening inside it.

vs Simple: Simple has no window at all — an untick is a second press with no clock behind it, so a mis-tap in a shop is only recoverable by hunting for the row again.

Study D — the register band (density only)

Claim: II.6.13's band table gives a working list Standard, 2rem. This screen is operated one-handed with a trolley in the other, so the Floor's 44px is a floor and 56px is the target. The cost is real and it is paid in rows-on-screen: at 390×844 the same bay seats 6 rows Dense, 4 Standard, 3 at 56px.

vs Simple: Simple's rows are whatever the text needs, which measured 26px — under the Floor on the one axis a thumb actually uses.

Rung 4 · Signature

The one move only this language would make: §7 removes the button's own outline and makes the whole plate the press — “the object that presses and the object that reports are the same shape” — and §4's Trophy clause removes the row itself once it has nothing to report. Press a row below. It commits at once, the enamel core fires for one frame, the flow gauge fills whole and tapers to zero over 1800ms on the taper curve, and then the row is simply not rendered. The lid's count is derived, and the clip confesses its remainder as a number.

What this rung is not allowed to do

Live appears nowhere except a window that is genuinely open — not on the till, not on a chip, not on an idle plate (§9, live creep). The run-out never ends in a single frame (§9, slam-shut). A press against a running window does nothing (§9, forced hold). And nothing renders on an unpressed row but its own dome and its own facts (§9, the over-decorated plate).
