FD-5 · STORES — the escalation ladder

10 CLEAR LID. Four rungs, each kept as a working artifact rather than a picture of one. Real data throughout, read off the running screen on 2026-08-18 against the real 65-row register. No image assets, no WebGL, no network.

Rung 1 · Simple

The doctrine's own grayscale ramp, #141414 to #F2F2F2. No gradient, no shadow stack, no bloom, no texture, no spring — values snap frame to frame and only contact acknowledges a touch. This rung exists to prove one thing: the task completes with the material argument entirely absent. Every state below is DRIVEN, not drawn: the selector changes what the component is given and the component renders whatever that is.

Verified on this rung: sixty-five real rows grouped by location, the longest real name, an expired row, an expiring row, rows that have NEVER been counted printing never rather than 0, a row whose shelf life carries no number printing low, the clipped remainder confessed as a figure, and a five-seat wheel that sets the addressed row. Everything above this rung buys a better report of the same facts — never a different fact.

Rung 2 · Pushed

Five studies. Each moves exactly one axis and leaves the others at the Simple rung, so each claim can be tested on its own rather than as part of a general improvement.

Study A — the plate (structure)

Claim. A register is ONE MACHINED OBJECT with rows milled into it, not sixty-five little cases. CLEAR LID names the alternative as its own failure mode — section 9, “LID INFLATION … the screen reads as a cabinet of specimen cases with no leading object” — and a 65-row register is the exact shape that invites it. Structure only; both sides stay grayscale and carry the identical text.

Against Simple: Simple has no container at all, so it cannot fail this way and cannot pass it either. A1 is not merely busier — it is a different claim about what the thing IS: sixty-five separately-lit objects sitting near each other. A2 says one object, and the grooves are painted into the plate's own background, so sixty-five rows cost one paint and no shadow layers at all (the figure beside them is counted off computed style in this page, not asserted). A2 kept, and it is the structure the Signature rung is built on.

Study B — the lid (where the tint sits)

Claim. CLEAR LID section 1 is “every value sits visible under a cool tint at rest; operating it costs the one gesture that clears the tint away” — so the addressed row must be the one WITHOUT tint over it. The naive reading lays a LIT pane over the addressed row, which puts the row the operator is working on under the most film on screen. One axis: where the acrylic is. Both sides use the same committed hexes.

Against Simple: Simple's addressed row is a border and reads at the plate's own contrast. B1 costs contrast exactly where the task is — the figures measured beside it are computed in this page by compositing the committed tint over the committed ground, not quoted. B2 costs nothing, because the pane covers the MECHANISM and never the printing, which is also what the source object does: the hood sits over the platter and the scale, and the chassis markings are outside it. B2 kept.

Study C — the pointer (colour)

Claim. CLEAR LID is a LIGHT world whose one emissive hue sits at almost exactly the luminance of its own paper, so amber cannot be the mark — it has to be the light INSIDE the mark. Colour only; the geometry, the well and the bloom stack are identical on both.

Against Simple: Simple's pointer is #F2F2F2 on #2c2c2c and reads at 11:1 without trying — a grayscale world has no such problem. In CLEAR LID the bare amber measures barely above its own well and the instrument disappears. The repair is precedented in this product: tokens/world.css does the same for RESTOMOD (“the pinned livery orange measures 2.89:1 on ivory … so the needle reads by its KEYLINE”). C2 kept, on every pointer on the screen.

Study D — the band (density)

Claim. CLEAR LID section 4's List/Browser bands are Dense 1.5rem, Standard 2rem, Comfortable 2.5rem. Every row on this screen is an OPERABLE CONTROL, and the Floor's 44px on both axes is non-negotiable, so the row is 2.75rem and the band table is overruled. Density only; the material is identical across all three.

Against Simple: Simple's rows are whatever the text makes them, so a long name is taller than a short one and the list has no pitch at all — which also means the milled grooves have nothing to be painted to. Watch D1 and D2: the 44px seat plate overflows its own row and the grooves stop lining up, which is the band table failing in public rather than in an argument. D3 kept, and it is what makes one repeating gradient able to engrave sixty-five rows.

Study E — the window (motion)

Claim. CLEAR LID section 3: the fenestrated cap's window is “sharp only when the knob rests on a detent, blurred whenever the hand is mid-turn, so the eye is never asked to overread a number the hand has not finished choosing”, and its Signature escalation delays the sharpening by 24ms behind the seat so the hand feels the detent before the eye reads the figure. Motion only; the cap is the same part as everywhere else.

Against Simple: Simple's five keys change a value with no travel at all, so a committed seat and a mis-tap look identical. Here the cap seats in 110ms on the snap curve, the window's blur tracks REAL angular velocity (min(3, ω/40)px), and the figure sharpens 24ms after the cap arrives. Turn it fast and the digit is deliberately unreadable mid-throw — which is the point. Kept, with the delta declared: the chapter re-cuts the Foundry's thirteen seats at 22.5° to THREE at 30°; the inventory level is five-valued, so this is FIVE seats at the chapter's own 30° pitch over 120°. Pitch, hysteresis (12%) and seat time (110ms) are unchanged.

Rung 4 · Signature

The tuning scale is geared to the thumbwheel. Every other product renders “what can I cook from stock” as five chips with a percentage each. This screen renders it as the radio it is — meals are printed STATIONS along one scale, the amber lamp edge-lights it from 26% of its width, and the pointer parks on the station you are tuned to. And it MOVES WHEN YOU COUNT, because changing what is in the house genuinely changes what tonight could be.

Set a level below and watch the order: the seat commits at once, the row's needle sweeps at the committed 340px/s, and the station marks and the tuning needle travel to wherever the coverage actually moved to. Nothing waits on anything — all three are reports of the same one commit, and the log prints the real arithmetic rather than the claim.

CD-BRIEF ruling 6 is satisfied by construction: this motion is user-caused. Leave the page alone and the scale is dead still — the lamp decays from its 120ms charge to a standing 34% ember over 6000ms and nothing else moves, because “a stable station, a stable speed, produces a stable screen” (II.4.16). There is no idle spin, no hover animation and no reveal with nothing behind it.

What this rung is not allowed to do

The station marks may only move when the coverage arithmetic moves. They are computed here from the same shape the frozen selector uses — a grams-weighted coverage in which each ingredient's contribution is capped at 100%, so a surplus of one cannot mask a shortage of another — and the pointer's travel time is distance over a stated rate with an 80ms floor, never a fixed tween. A geometry change (a resize, a first measurement) relocates every needle in one frame instead, because a sweep is a report of a VALUE change and of nothing else.
