FD-5 · TODAY — the escalation ladder

05 EXPOSED WORKS. Four rungs, each kept as a working artifact rather than a picture of one. Real data throughout, read off the running app on 2026-08-17 in morrisons-tester mode. No image assets, no WebGL, no network.

Rung 1 · Simple

The doctrine's own grayscale ramp, #141414 to #F2F2F2. No gradient, no shadow stack, no bloom, no texture, no spring — values snap frame to frame and only contact acknowledges a touch. This rung exists to prove one thing: the task completes with the material argument entirely absent. Every state below is DRIVEN, not drawn — the selector really changes what the component is given, and the component renders whatever that is.

Verified on this rung: the arbiter's one act-now line, the start-by figure, the eaten figure against its band, three tickable slots and one cut slot, the duty list with its overflow confessed as a number, and an age that says never rather than 0 when a reading was never taken. Everything above this rung buys a better report of the same facts — never a different fact.

Rung 2 · Pushed

Four studies. Each moves exactly one axis and leaves the other three at the Simple rung, so each claim can be tested on its own rather than as part of a general improvement.

Study A — the cluster (geometry)

Claim. Two primary dials with a subordinate row beneath them read faster than four equal cells, because rank is legible before any value is parsed. Geometry alone, still grayscale, still no material.

Against Simple: Simple's two cells are the same size and the same shape, so the eye has to read both before it knows which one is the answer. Here the ratio is legible at a glance and the subordinate row is visibly evidence. Kept — this is the composition that shipped, with the chronometer taking the first seat.

Study B — the register (density)

Claim. The duty deck belongs in II.6.13's Standard band, 2.75rem rows, because a duty is decided from rather than merely read, and because 2.75rem is also the Floor's 44px target — one number serving two laws.

Against Simple: Simple has no band at all — its rows are whatever the text makes them, so a row with a long ingredient name is taller than one without and the list has no rhythm. Dense 2rem cannot hold a 44px key. Comfortable 3.5rem pushes the second lid off the fold at 800px. Standard kept.

Study C — the leather spine (material)

Claim. Four materials, keyed to ch.05's section-2 registry and built from II.2's recipes, read as four different substances rather than four tints: carbon twill at a 32px cell, satin billet with 3px linear grain, a cognac-lined field, and cream lacquer whose whole story is one hard reflection ellipse. Geometry stays exactly as Simple's.

The push that did NOT survive: a porthole on every ladder as well as both dials, which read as decoration the moment nothing was turning behind six of them.

Study D — the linkage (motion)

Claim. A rocker arm that swings the detent's own 22.5° pitch a beat behind the cap reports the click twice — once to the hand, once to the eye — and a mid-throw reversal must not compound it into 45°. Motion only; the parts stay grayscale.

Against Simple: Simple's value snaps and nothing moves, so a click and a no-op look identical. Drive this one forward twice and back twice: the readout returns to exactly −33.75°, never −67.5°, because the cap and the rocker are siblings on a carrier that holds no transform of its own. Nest one inside the other and each click renders 45°. Kept, and it is the mechanism the Signature rung is built on.

Rung 4 · Signature

ch.05 section 7: "the hero gauge's own porthole disc is geared 1:1 to the same rocker arm that clicks the mode knob two panels away, so turning the knob makes the hero gauge's mechanism visibly answer BEFORE the needle finishes its sweep." Built exactly that, and nothing else: one commit angle, written once, read independently by the cap, the rocker and the porthole's geared disc. The disc seats in 150ms — 110ms snap plus the rocker's own 40ms beat — and the needle takes up to 375ms at the project's 720°/s. The mechanism answers first by arithmetic, not by an animation ordered to look that way.

Drive it and watch the order: the rocker leaves at once, the disc turns exactly one tooth per detent — the teeth are cut at 22.5°, the same pitch — and the needle sweeps at the product's fixed rate. The log beneath prints the real arithmetic rather than the claim: the disc seats at 150ms, the needle takes 80–375ms, so the mechanism finishes first whenever the value moves more than about 108° of arc, and below that the two are simultaneous. Both begin in the same task either way — nothing waits on anything.

What this rung is not allowed to do

The disc turns only while the value turns. There is no idle spin, no hover animation and no motion with nothing behind it — CD-BRIEF ruling 6 attaches portholes and rocker arms only to values whose change is user-caused or clock-continuous, and this one is user-caused. On the shipped screen the second porthole is an escapement that steps one tooth per minute consumed, and it steps for that reason and no other.
