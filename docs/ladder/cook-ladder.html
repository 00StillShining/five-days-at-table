FD-5 COOK — 02 REEL LOGIC — the escalation ladder

Four rungs, all live. Real data from the running app, 2026-08-18.

COOK answers what am I doing right now, and when does it need me? REEL LOGIC owns it because the cook timer is the one genuinely live process in the whole product — everywhere else FD-5's data changes once a day, and CD-BRIEF ruling 6 says spinning elements attach only to values whose change is user-caused or clock-continuous. A disc that would be ornament on any other screen is the honest instrument here.

1 · Simple

Grayscale ramp #141414 → #F2F2F2, nothing else. No gradient, no shadow stack, no bloom, no texture, no material, no motion. The disc does not exist yet: elapsed is a number, not a reel. The task still completes — read the state, read what is left, press the one key that advances it — which is the whole point of the rung.

Every state, including the four this screen adds: running, paused, stopped, overdue.

the state set

Thirteen states, each real: the nine the ladder asks for plus the four a timer adds. Running is DRIVEN, not drawn — the figure below counts down against a real clock while it is selected.

2 · Pushed — four studies, ONE exaggerated axis each

Each study pushes exactly one axis to the point where it argues, states its claim, and is compared against Simple. None of them is the answer; each of them tells you something Simple could not.

study A — geometry: the Ø18rem reel hero

Claim. The chapter's Hero Screen puts one disc at Ø18rem with nothing competing beside it. If the disc is big enough, its stillness alone carries the alarm and the printed words become confirmation rather than the report.

vs Simple: the state is now readable across a room, and a stopped disc is felt before it is read. What it costs: the deck answers only half its own question. "When does it need me" is legible; "what am I doing right now" is nowhere, and the step text is the thing the operator's hands are actually following. Kept: the diameter, and the disc as the screen's first mass. Rejected: the disc alone.

study B — motion: the transport folded into the reel's own gesture grammar

Claim. The chapter's Signature removes the key row and folds every transport function into the disc: tap centre to play, radial flick to seek, 350ms hold to arm. One control becomes the input, the readout and the transport.

vs Simple: it would be the most economical control on the screen. Why it is refused: FD-5's ruling is that this reel is an aria-hidden process readout and is never grabbed. That is not a taste call — it is the single reason the FACETED VOLUME × REEL LOGIC curdle does not exist in this product: "both fuse a rotary and its own readout into one control, but one earns coast through traversal and the other refuses coast on principle." Make this disc grabbable and MEAL's wheel and COOK's reel become two contradictory laws in one product. Kept: the Signature's claim — no transport key row anywhere. Rejected: the grab that delivers it.

study C — density: lane-dominant

Claim. The real work of a Sunday session is eight parallel tracks. Give the lanes the deck and the reel a corner, and the screen answers "what is cooking where" better than any disc can.

vs Simple: the parallel structure of a prep session becomes visible for the first time — Simple's one bar cannot show that the oven and the pan are different clocks. What it costs: at eight lanes the deck stands 1005px in a 768px viewport, and nothing tells you which lane needs you now. Kept: the lanes, their three-zone windows and the shared playhead — and the measurement that forced the ranking and the cap. Rejected: lanes as the hero.

study D — palette: lamp-only saturation

Claim. The chapter grants one bulb per world. Spend every saturated pixel on the record lamp and let the rest of the deck be chrome and ink, so the one colour on screen always means exactly one thing.

vs Simple: "is it recording" becomes a pre-attentive fact. What it costs: with saturation rationed to the lamp, an overdue step has no colour left — and the alarm is the one thing on this screen that must survive a glance from two metres. Kept: the lamp as the only emissive, and its word beside it, because a colour never travels alone. Rejected: the ration. The danger role is spent as a printed keyline and a filled meter as well, which is pigment, not light.

4 · Signature — the one move only this screen would make

THE TRANSPORT IS THE REEL'S OWN RIM. There is no transport key row anywhere on this screen. The three keys are seated in the machined collar that is part of the reel assembly — hold at 9, done at 6, +1 min at 3 — with the record lamp on the crown at 12 and the count printed around the rim as II.3.18's committed 270° arc, its 90° bottom dead zone occupied by the primary key. One object is the input, the readout and the transport, reached by pressing the assembly's rim rather than by turning its disc.

The tension, stated rather than hidden. The chapter delivers this by making the disc grabbable, and FD-5 forbids that (study B). So the Signature is built as the chapter means it — the key row is gone, the reel carries everything — and not as the chapter draws it. That is a real departure and it is recorded here rather than smoothed over.

step due

Press the collar. HOLD freezes the disc in the input frame — the commit is applied to a shadow model with the frozen reducer and the disc's own attribute is written straight to the DOM, before the handler returns. DONE clears the alarm. The disc stops for exactly three reasons and every one of them is printed: HOLD, WAIT, READY.
